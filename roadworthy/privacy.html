The short version
  • Photographs never leave your device. Number plates are read on the phone itself.
  • There is no account. Nothing to log in to, and no profile of you anywhere.
  • Tax and vehicle details go directly from your device to DVLA's public service.
  • MOT history is the one exception: it passes through a small server we operate, which receives the registration and nothing else. Why, and what is kept, is set out below. The same server is also asked, with nothing about you or a vehicle, whether to offer a partner for booking an MOT on a car of your own.
  • If you report a vehicle for having no MOT, your coordinates alone go to two public services to find the right police force. Your registration does not.
  • Advertising is separate, asks for your consent, and never sees vehicle data.

Roadworthy is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or operated by the DVLA, DVSA, the Motor Insurers' Bureau, or any government body.

Your camera

The camera is optional. You can type a registration instead and refuse the permission outright. Every screen that offers the camera also offers a field, so nothing in the app is behind that prompt.

If you do use it, plate recognition runs entirely on your device using Google ML Kit. Images are never uploaded, never stored, and never sent anywhere. Only the registration you confirm is used, and only to perform a lookup.

Lawful basis: consent. You grant the permission, and the app works without it.

Tax and vehicle details

When you check a vehicle, your device contacts DVLA's public Vehicle Enquiry Service (vehicleenquiry.service.gov.uk) directly to retrieve tax status, make, colour and specification. That request comes from your device and your connection; it does not pass through us.

Information published there is used under the Open Government Licence v3.0:

Contains public sector information licensed under the Open Government Licence v3.0.

Lawful basis: legitimate interests (providing the check you asked for)

MOT history

MOT test history comes from DVSA's MOT History API. Reaching that service requires credentials, and credentials built into an app can be extracted from it, so this one request passes through a small server we operate, which holds the credentials and forwards the lookup on your behalf.

That server receives the registration and nothing else. No photograph, no location, no account, no device identifier and no advertising identifier. It keeps no database of vehicles and builds no history of what you have looked up.

One retention point, stated plainly rather than buried: our hosting provider (Cloudflare) records the address of each incoming request so we can see the service is working, and that address contains the registration. Those records are deleted automatically after three days. They exist so we can notice a fault (for example DVSA changing something) before it affects you. They are not linked to you, your device or your advertising identifier, and are never used to build a profile.

Lawful basis: legitimate interests (providing the check you asked for, and keeping the service working)

Vehicles you save

Saving a vehicle stores its full check (registration, make, model, colour, tax and MOT status, specification and MOT history) only on your device, so you can open it again without another lookup. It is not backed up to us and not synced. Deleting a vehicle, or the app, removes it.

Your recent checks

The app keeps a list of vehicles you have checked recently so you can find them again from the home screen. This list is held only on your device, is limited to the 50 most recent checks, and older entries are discarded automatically as new ones arrive.

You can clear it at any time in the app, and uninstalling removes it. Nothing in it is sent to us.

Notifications, and starting when your phone does

The app can remind you before the MOT and tax on your own vehicles run out. It holds two permissions for that.

  • Show notifications. Asked for only when you turn reminders on for a car you have said is yours, or tap a reminders switch while notifications are off, never when the app opens. Refusing it changes nothing else in the app, and a car's reminders are only turned on once notifications are allowed.
  • Run at startup. This lets the once-a-day reminder check carry on after your phone restarts. That check reads only the dates already saved on your phone; it contacts no server and sends nothing anywhere. Android grants this permission to the whole app, not to one feature, so a task the advertising library has left waiting may also carry on after a restart. What that library sends is set out under Advertising.

You can turn reminders off for one car on its own screen, or for every car in Settings.

Reminders are worked out on your phone, only for vehicles you have saved and said are yours, and only where you asked for them. To avoid sending the same reminder twice, the app records on your phone which ones it has sent: the registration, which reminder, and the expiry date it was about. That record is deleted from the phone as soon as you remove the vehicle. So that Undo can put it back, the app keeps a copy in memory only, never written, until another vehicle is removed or the app closes. Nothing in it is sent to us.

Location

Your device's location is optional. If you allow it, your device's position is converted to a street and town so a report can be completed; you can also type the address yourself and refuse the permission entirely.

Your location is used at that moment and is not stored or transmitted to us. It is included in a report only if you submit one.

For a no-MOT report only, your coordinates are also sent from your device to two public services, to work out which police force covers that place:

  • postcodes.io, to establish whether it is in England, Wales, Scotland or Northern Ireland.
  • data.police.uk, the Home Office's own service, to identify the covering force.

Both receive a latitude and longitude and nothing else: no registration, no identifier, and nothing that links the request to you or to the vehicle. Neither request happens unless you open a no-MOT report, and the app falls back to a national page if they fail. Both are free public services published under the Open Government Licence.

Lawful basis: consent. You grant the permission, and the app works without it

Reporting a vehicle

If you choose to report a vehicle, the details you have reviewed on screen go from your device to the relevant authority. Nothing is submitted without you acting, and we never submit on your behalf.

The two routes work differently, and the app tells you which one you are on:

  • Untaxed goes to DVLA, submitted from the app when you tap submit.
  • No MOT goes to the police, because DVLA and DVSA cannot act on a missing MOT. The app copies the details to your clipboard and opens your police force's own reporting form in your browser. You fill it in and submit it. Nothing is sent by the app.

Those submissions are governed by the receiving authority's own privacy notice, and once you are on a police website you are subject to that force's. DVLA's untaxed vehicle reports are anonymous to the vehicle keeper.

We are not told whether your report was accepted. It goes from your device to the authority, and no copy reaches us.

Partner links

On a car you have said is yours, when its MOT is due or has run out, the app may offer to book the MOT with a partner, Fixter. The offer is labelled "Partner · Ad", and says we are paid a commission if you book.

  • Whether to offer a partner is asked of our server, the same one MOT history passes through. While one of your cars has that MOT card on screen, and at most once a day, the app asks it which partner, if any, to show. That request contains nothing about you or any vehicle: no registration and no identifier. Like any request it comes from your internet address and names your phone's Android version and model, and our hosting provider records it for three days, as described under MOT history. That record holds no registration.
  • If you tap the partner's button, your browser opens the partner's site through a tracking link run by Awin, the affiliate network that pays us, so that a booking can be credited to us. The app adds nothing to that link: you type the registration and your postcode on the partner's own site. From there Awin and the partner receive what any website receives from your browser, and use cookies, under their own privacy notices: Awin's and Fixter's. Awin says it "doesn't know who the end user is". We are not given your name or contact details.

If you never tap the button, nothing goes to Awin or the partner.

Lawful basis: legitimate interests (offering a way to book an MOT, and paying for a free app)

Insurance checks

Selecting "Check insurance" opens the Motor Insurers' Bureau's own service inside the app. We do not perform the check and do not see the result. You interact with MIB directly and their privacy notice applies. This is offered only for vehicles you have told us are yours, because MIB permit it only in that case.

Advertising

The app shows advertising supplied by Google AdMob.

Advertising is a separate purpose from vehicle checking, and asks separately for your consent. In the UK and EEA you are shown a consent form before any advertising identifier is collected.

  • If you consent, Google may use your device's advertising identifier and similar identifiers to choose and measure ads, including ads based on your interests.
  • If you do not consent, you still see ads, but they are not personalised. Google calls these "limited ads": it says they use no identifier kept on your device to choose them (Google's explanation). Google says it may still store data on your device solely to detect fraud and invalid traffic.
  • If the form cannot be shown at all, with no connection, say, and you have never answered it, the advertising software is not started and no ads are shown.
  • If Google's software finds that no question is needed where your phone is, outside the United Kingdom and Europe, for example, no form is shown, and ads are shown as Google ordinarily shows them there.

Whenever the advertising software is running, Google receives information from the app, which it says it uses for advertising, analytics and fraud prevention. Google's published description lists: your internet address, which Google says may be used to estimate the general location of a device; identifiers, as above; how you interact with the app and its ads, such as launches and taps; and diagnostic information such as launch time (Google's description). See Google's privacy policy for what it does with this.

Advertising never receives vehicle data. No registration, make, model, colour, report content or location you give for a report is shared with advertisers or used for ad targeting. This is enforced in the code, not merely as a policy: the advertising component cannot access vehicle data, and an automated test fails the build if that separation is broken.

You can change or withdraw your choice at any time through Advertising privacy options in the app's Settings, which opens from the ⋮ menu.

Lawful basis: consent for personalised ads and for anything stored on or read from your device to choose them; legitimate interests for showing non-personalised ads to pay for a free app, and for detecting fraud and invalid traffic.

Your rights

Roadworthy is available in the United Kingdom and Ireland. In the UK, UK GDPR gives you rights of access, correction, erasure, restriction and objection. In Ireland, the EU General Data Protection Regulation gives you the same rights.

If you are unhappy with how your data is handled, you can complain to the data protection regulator: the Information Commissioner's Office in the UK, or the Data Protection Commission in Ireland. We would welcome the chance to put it right first, so please contact us too.

We hold no account and no profile of you, so most of these are exercised directly on your device: delete saved vehicles or clear your recent checks in the app, revoke permissions in Android settings, withdraw advertising consent through the app's privacy options, or uninstall.

The only record we hold anywhere else is the three-day request log described under MOT history (and, without a registration, under Partner links). It contains a registration and no identifier for you, so we generally cannot connect it to a person in order to answer a request about it, but if you believe an entry relates to you, contact us and we will help where we can.

For data held by DVLA, DVSA or MIB, contact them directly: they are the controllers of their own records.

Children

Roadworthy is not directed at children and should not be used by anyone under 13.

Changes to this policy

If the data flows change, this page changes with them and the date above is updated.

The previous version of this page said the most likely such change would be lookups moving to a server we operate. That has now happened, for MOT history only, and is described above. Tax and vehicle details still go directly from your device to DVLA.
