## Free government checks

### F1. What DVLA's vehicle enquiry service shows

Source: GOV.UK, Get vehicle information from DVLA,
<https://www.gov.uk/get-vehicle-information-from-dvla>

Lists: "vehicle tax - the current rate and when it expires", "SORN status", "when its MOT
expires", "the date it was first registered", "last log book (V5C) issue date", "year of
manufacture", "type approval category", "weight", "engine size", "fuel type", "emissions - CO2,
real driving emissions level and European status", "export status".

It does not mention finance, theft or write-off status.

### F2. What DVSA's MOT history shows, and how far back

Source: GOV.UK, Check the MOT history of a vehicle, <https://www.gov.uk/check-mot-history>

Shows "if it passed or failed", "the mileage recorded when it was tested", "when the next MOT
is due". For England, Scotland and Wales, also "what parts failed at each test, and if any parts
had minor problems".

Coverage: cars, motorcycles and vans in England, Scotland and Wales, "tests done since 2005";
HGVs, trailers, buses and coaches "since 2018"; Northern Ireland "tests done since 2017".

### F3. What the MOT test does not cover

Source: GOV.UK, Getting an MOT: the MOT test, <https://www.gov.uk/getting-an-mot/the-mot-test>

"The test does not cover the condition of the engine, clutch or gearbox."

## GOV.UK's used-car checklist

### F4. Check a used vehicle you're buying

Source: GOV.UK, <https://www.gov.uk/checks-when-buying-a-used-car>

Before seeing the vehicle:
- "Ask the seller for the registration number, make and model and MOT test number."
- "Check that the details you've been given match the information held by DVLA."
- "Check the MOT status and history matches the details you've been given."
- "Check if the vehicle has been recalled because of a serious safety issue."

When seeing the vehicle:
- "Ask to see the V5C vehicle registration certificate ('log book'). Make sure it has a 'DVL'
  watermark, and the serial number is not between BG8229501 to BG9999030, or BI2305501 to
  BI2800000."
- "If it is, the V5C might be stolen - call the police as soon as it's safe to."
- "Make sure the details in the log book match the details you've been given."
- "Check the vehicle identification number and engine number. Make sure these match the details
  on the log book."

After buying:
- "Tax the vehicle immediately. You'll need the green 'new keeper' slip from the vehicle's log
  book."

The guide does not mention paid history checks, finance or write-offs.

## Finance

### F5. Who owns a car on hire purchase or conditional sale

Source: Hire Purchase Act 1964, section 29 (definitions),
<https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1964/53/section/29>

Hire-purchase agreement: goods are bailed (or in Scotland hired) "in return for periodical
payments" and "the property in the goods will pass to that person if the terms of the agreement
are complied with".

Conditional sale agreement: "the property in the goods is to remain in the seller ... until such
conditions as to the payment of instalments or otherwise as may be specified in the agreement
are fulfilled."

Private purchaser: "a purchaser who, at the time of the disposition made to him, does not carry
on any such business" (that is, not a trade or finance purchaser).

Trade or finance purchaser: one who "carries on a business which consists, wholly or partly,
(a) of purchasing motor vehicles for the purpose of offering or exposing them for sale, or (b) of
providing finance by purchasing motor vehicles ...".

### F6. Protection for a private buyer of a car with outstanding finance

Source: Hire Purchase Act 1964, section 27,
<https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1964/53/section/27>

Applies where a motor vehicle on hire purchase or conditional sale is disposed of by the debtor
before the property has passed to them. Where the disposition is "to a private purchaser, and he
is a purchaser of the motor vehicle in good faith without notice" of the agreement, it takes
effect as if the creditor's title had vested in the debtor immediately before.

In force. Extent: England, Wales and Scotland (E+W+S). Not Northern Ireland.

Page wording built on this: the protection is limited to a private buyer acting in good faith
without notice; it does not cover trade or finance purchasers. "Can still mean a dispute with the
lender" is our plain-English reading, not a quote.

## Stolen vehicles

### F7. No better title than the seller had

Source: Sale of Goods Act 1979, section 21(1),
<https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1979/54/section/21>

"Subject to this Act, where goods are sold by a person who is not their owner, and who does not
sell them under the authority or with the consent of the owner, the buyer acquires no better
title to the goods than the seller had, unless the owner of the goods is by his conduct precluded
from denying the seller's authority to sell."

Extent: United Kingdom.

## Write-offs

### F8. Insurance write-off categories

Source: GOV.UK, Insurance write-offs,
<https://www.gov.uk/scrapped-and-written-off-vehicles/insurance-writeoffs>

- Category A: "Cannot be repaired"; "Entire vehicle has to be crushed."
- Category B: "Cannot be repaired"; the body shell is crushed and "you can salvage other parts
  from it".
- Category S: "Can be repaired following structural damage"; "You can use the vehicle again if
  it's repaired to a roadworthy condition."
- Category N: "Can be repaired following non-structural damage"; "You can use the vehicle again
  if it's repaired to a roadworthy condition."

Category S kept by the owner: "DVLA will record the vehicle's category in the log book."
Category N: "You can keep the log book if you want to keep a category N vehicle."

Owners must "Tell DVLA your vehicle has been written off"; "You can be fined £1,000 if you do not
tell DVLA."

Background (secondary sources, not cited on the page): the ABI changed its salvage code of
practice in October 2017, replacing categories C and D with S and N.

## Mileage

### F9. Misleading mileage and traders

Source: Business Companion (Trading Standards guidance), Mileage of used vehicles,
<https://www.businesscompanion.info/en/quick-guides/goods/mileage-of-used-vehicles>
(page last updated April 2025)

Under the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024, an offence includes "making a
misleading statement by any means. This could include altering an odometer reading (for example,
from a higher to lower figure or to zero)".

"The offences contained in the DMCCA would apply even if a trader did not know that the indicated
mileage was incorrect." Defence: the trader "took all reasonable precautions and exercised all
due diligence".

Not sourced, and kept off the page as a legal claim: anything about private sellers' liability.

### F10. Reading mileage in the MOT history (our reasoning, not a source)

F2 establishes that each test records the mileage. That a falling reading is "worth asking
about", and that a replaced instrument cluster or a recording error can explain one, is general
reasoning, written as advice rather than as a sourced fact.

## Paid history checks

### F11. What paid checks cover, and roughly what they cost

Not a government source. Providers' own descriptions and comparison sites (October 2026):
paid checks search finance agreements, insurer write-off records and police stolen markers,
which the free services do not show (see F1, F2, F4).

Single-check prices seen in October 2026: HPI about £19.99 (3 for £29.99); Total Car Check Gold
£9.99; Instant Car Check £5.99 Gold, with outstanding finance as a £4.99 add-on; carVertical
about £32 to £40 before discounts. The page says "roughly £10 to £20" for the best-known
providers and warns that some leave finance out of the base price.

These prices change often. Re-check before quoting a figure, and prefer no figure to a stale one.

Comparison pages used: <https://www.caranalytics.co.uk/guides/who-has-the-cheapest-hpi-check/>,
<https://carhealth.co.uk/blog/carvertical-vs-hpi-check>, <https://totalcarcheck.co.uk/Trade>.
Several comparison sites are run by competing check providers; treat them as indicative only.
